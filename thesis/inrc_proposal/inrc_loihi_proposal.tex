\textbf{Objective.} Build a \emph{spiking diagonal state-space} object detector for event cameras and map it onto
\textbf{Intel Loihi 2}, targeting the size-weight-and-power (SWaP) budget of micro-UAVs. The spiking
model is the new work. It is built as a controlled swap into a \emph{completed and fully benchmarked}
non-spiking reference pipeline, so the experiment measures one thing cleanly: \textbf{what does spiking a
state-space detector actually cost, and what does neuromorphic hardware give back?}

%---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The gap we propose to close}
On Prophesee Gen1 --- the standard event-camera detection benchmark --- directly-trained SNN detectors trail
conventional ANN detectors by roughly \textbf{6--7 mAP}. Our own non-spiking models sit at the top of the ANN
group, which puts us in an unusually direct position to attack that gap from above.

\vspace{-4pt}
The accuracy-recovery mechanisms that demonstrably work for SNN detectors are \textbf{recurrence} and
\textbf{richer information per spike}. A state-space neuron supplies the first natively, and Loihi 2's
\textbf{graded spikes} supply the second. That is the hypothesis: \emph{a spiking SSM should narrow the SNN
accuracy gap precisely because its neuron model already is a temporal filter.}

%---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{What is already built (the control arm)}
We reproduced the S5-RVT baseline exactly (Gen1 test AP $=47.7$) and built two original detectors as
\emph{controlled} backbone swaps into that verified pipeline --- identical neck (YOLO-PAFPN), head (YOLOX),
loss, data pipeline and evaluator, so only the backbone changes. Beyond accuracy we measured, on a calibrated
idle-GPU-guarded harness: PureSSM is the \textbf{smallest} model (16.3\,M), does the \textbf{least compute}
(10.1\,GFLOP) with the \textbf{best accuracy-per-FLOP} (4.59 mAP/GFLOP), and is the \textbf{most rate-robust}
--- retaining \textbf{69.7\,\%} of its accuracy under a true $10\times$ change in event rate, against 17.7\,\%
for a ConvLSTM baseline.

This matters to the present proposal for one reason: it is a \emph{frozen, instrumented harness}. Dropping a
spiking backbone into it isolates the cost of spiking with no confounds --- a controlled spiking-vs-non-spiking
comparison that, to our knowledge, no published SNN detector paper provides.

%---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Why Loihi 2 specifically}
Three measured properties of our detector each map to a concrete Loihi 2 mechanism.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=1.5pt, topsep=1.5pt]
  \item \textbf{The temporal core is a diagonal linear recurrence} $x_k=\bar{A}x_{k-1}+\bar{B}u_k,\;y_k=Cx_k$ ---
  a bank of independent scalar states. Loihi 2's \textbf{programmable neuron microcode} implements exactly this
  class of dynamics natively, rather than forcing it into a fixed LIF primitive. Meyer \emph{et al.}~[1] mapped
  a diagonal SSM (S4D) to Loihi 2 and measured ${\sim}1000\times$ lower energy and ${\sim}75\times$ lower latency
  than the same model on an NVIDIA Jetson --- direct feasibility evidence for the mapping we propose.
  \item \textbf{Event sparsity is wasted on a GPU but rewarded on Loihi 2.} We measured that sparse event input
  yields \emph{no} GPU speed-up --- dense kernels process idle pixels regardless. On an event-driven substrate
  idle pixels cost no energy, so the sparsity we cannot exploit becomes a direct advantage. The foundational
  1\,Mpx event-detection paper names neuromorphic hardware as precisely the route to exploit the sparsity its
  own dense pipeline wastes~[4].
  \item \textbf{Graded spikes address the binary bottleneck.} The dominant accuracy cost in SNN detection is the
  binary spike's information limit. Loihi 2's graded (integer-valued) spikes are the hardware analogue of the
  trick that lifted SpikeYOLO to SNN SOTA~[6] --- so the accuracy recovery is a Loihi 2 capability, not a
  software workaround.
\end{enumerate}

Combined with PureSSM's rate-robustness --- a \emph{single} deployed network handling the variable event rates of
flight without retraining --- this is what moves a GPU cost of ${\sim}0.65$\,J/frame toward the milliwatt envelope a
sub-250\,g UAV can carry, a budget no desktop GPU or embedded module meets.

%---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Approach and milestones}
We target a \textbf{fixed-$\Delta t$ diagonal S5/S4D recurrence}. Mamba's input-dependent $\Delta t$ is the one
component that does not map to Loihi 2's fixed per-neuron microcode, and our own two-regime rate study already
quantifies the small, bounded accuracy cost of dropping it. Following Intel's guidance to design locally first,
we build and train in PyTorch/snnTorch (surrogate-gradient SNN training), export through
\textbf{NIR} --- the vendor-neutral neuromorphic intermediate representation --- and benchmark on vLab before any
hardware request. \emph{As the Lava repositories were archived in May 2026, we treat NIR as the export
boundary so the model retargets cleanly to a successor SDK; guidance on Intel's preferred path is welcome.}

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Readiness against the INRC checklist --- stated plainly.}
(i) \emph{Project plan} --- this document.
(ii) \emph{Built and tested an SNN} --- \textbf{not yet met, and it is the substance of M1--M2.} Our present
models are ANNs; we take Intel's point that ANN and RNN experience does not by itself prepare a team for Loihi.
What we bring instead is a validated reference pipeline, a frozen evaluation harness, and a calibrated
energy/latency/FLOP benchmark, so the spiking model has a rigorous control to be measured against from day one.
(iii) \emph{Implemented in the Loihi toolchain} --- M3, via NIR.
(iv) \emph{Evaluated in simulation / on vLab} --- M3, and the gate on any hardware request.
\emph{This is why we request vLab access now rather than hardware:} the cloud is where we would build and
validate the SNN, and a hardware request follows only once M3 justifies it.

\vspace{2pt}
\textbf{Contributed back.} An open-source, NIR-exportable implementation of a spiking diagonal-SSM detector; the first
controlled measurement of the accuracy and energy cost of spiking an SSM detector; and a Gen1 benchmark point
that extends the INRC community's event-vision results. \textbf{Expected outcome:} the first spiking
state-space object detector for event cameras on Loihi 2.
