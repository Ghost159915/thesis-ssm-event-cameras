\textbf{Objective.} Develop a \emph{spiking diagonal state-space} object detector for event cameras and map it onto
\textbf{Intel Loihi 2} (via Lava), targeting the size-weight-and-power (SWaP) budget of micro-UAVs. The work extends
a \emph{completed} thesis in which we built and benchmarked two original state-space (SSM) event detectors on the
Prophesee Gen1 benchmark.

%---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Current results (completed)}
We reproduced the S5-RVT baseline exactly (Gen1 test AP $=47.7$) and built two original detectors as
\emph{controlled} backbone swaps into that verified pipeline --- identical neck, head, loss, data, and evaluator, so
only the backbone's spatial mixer changes. \textbf{EventSSM} uses ResNet-18 conv $+$ Mamba temporal recurrence;
\textbf{PureSSM} uses a pure bidirectional-Mamba spatial mixer $+$ the same temporal path. The pure state-space
model is the smallest, most compute-efficient, and most rate-robust of the three (table below).

\section{Why Loihi 2 benefits \emph{this} model}
The case is specific, not generic --- three measured properties of our detector each map to a concrete Loihi 2 gain:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{The temporal core is a diagonal linear recurrence} $x_k=\bar{A}x_{k-1}+\bar{B}u_k,\;y_k=Cx_k$ ---
  a bank of independent scalar states that maps \emph{natively} onto Loihi 2's programmable per-neuron dynamics.
  Meyer \emph{et al.} (2024) mapped exactly this class (S4D) to Loihi 2 and measured ${\sim}1000\times$ lower energy
  and ${\sim}75\times$ lower latency than an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano for streaming inference~[1].
  \item \textbf{Event sparsity is wasted on a GPU but rewarded on Loihi 2.} We measured that our model's sparse,
  event-driven input gives \emph{no} speed-up on GPU --- dense kernels process idle pixels anyway. On Loihi 2's
  event-driven substrate, idle pixels cost no energy, so the sparsity we cannot exploit on a GPU becomes a direct
  advantage. This is the clearest reason the same network is better served by neuromorphic hardware.
  \item \textbf{Rate-robustness $+$ leanness suit the flight envelope.} PureSSM keeps $69.7\%$ of its accuracy under
  a true $10\times$ event-rate change, so a \emph{single} deployed network handles the variable event rates of flight
  without retraining. Combined with its leanness and event-driven execution, this is what brings a GPU cost of
  ${\sim}0.65$\,J/frame toward the milliwatt envelope a sub-250\,g UAV can actually carry --- a budget no desktop GPU
  or embedded Jetson meets.
\end{enumerate}

%---------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Approach and plan}
We target a \textbf{fixed-$\Delta t$ diagonal S5/S4D recurrence}: Mamba's input-dependent $\Delta t$ is the one part
that does not map to Loihi 2's fixed per-neuron microcode, and our two-regime study already quantified the small,
bounded accuracy cost of dropping it. We implement the block and a lightweight event front-end in \textbf{Lava},
validate numerical equivalence against the PyTorch reference, and benchmark on the Neuromorphic Research Cloud
(vLab) before requesting on-chip hardware --- following Intel's guidance to design locally first.

\textbf{Readiness (Intel checklist).} Working detector, full reproducible pipeline, and a calibrated
energy/latency/FLOP benchmark harness are \emph{complete}; the immediate next step is the spiking model in Lava

spiking state-space object detector for event cameras on Loihi 2, with an energy--accuracy--latency characterisation
and an open Lava implementation.
